# Adaptive formal increments: observed application results

Nine fresh Agent threads completed their initial tasks and same-Agent continuations:
**nine independent cells, eighteen task stages**. All three conditions built on the
same application during continuation and preserved their earlier delivery. The
integrated method made scope, inheritance and remaining responsibility more explicit
in several records. The baseline and judgment-only conditions also produced coherent
increments and substantial visual repairs. These runs do not establish a general
visual-quality advantage, an efficiency gain or an optimal number of SPECs.

The [execution record](execution.json), [registered protocol](PROTOCOL.md),
[unchanged input registration](registration.json) and [dispatch events](run-events.json)
separate what was planned from what actually ran. The runtime released as v1.17.0
matches condition C's frozen 37-file input exactly. Later packaging, replay and
reporting changes do not silently substitute a different tested Skill.

## Scope and process observations

These are maintainer observations of source and participant records with conditions
known. They are separate from anonymous live-product review. The detailed
[process record](process-observations.json) identifies responsible files and limits.

### T1: retaining a coherent conventional workspace

All three conditions kept collection selection, editing, drafts, search and saving
together. None created isolated applications for the list and editor. Each reused
its chosen foundation when introducing shared density and bilingual typography.
C explicitly separated research, primary implementation and regression scope and
explained the collection/editor dependency. A and B also produced a sensible keep
decision without that explicit three-scope record.

This family supplies a counterexample to "smaller is always better": a conventional
workspace supported by mature foundations did not need gratuitous formal splitting.
Record clarity is observable; superior rendered quality cannot be inferred from it.
All three T1 continuations were interrupted by the host and resumed with approximate
remaining allowances. Their wall-clock intervals must not rank efficiency.

### T2: preserving the time-dependent conversation relationship

All three implementations connected composer, streaming content, scroll control,
history and source reading. Each continuation adapted the existing application.
C named SPEC-01 for the ask/read/inspect/continue loop and SPEC-02 for concurrent
inline citations. Its first source cards appeared after completion; the second
increment assumed a concrete new responsibility for reading a cited paragraph while
generation continues. A and B also implemented the coupled experience without
splitting input, messages and scrolling into separate products.

All three adapted the supplied native conversation foundation. A and B additionally
reported inspecting the upstream public empty composition, and B compared a sidebar
example. C explicitly disclosed that it did not operate the upstream React demo.
This family does **not** support a claim that C performed deeper reference research.
The supplied foundation is a native adaptation, not the upstream React runtime.

The brief already prioritized a usable core and supplied a relevant reference.
It therefore provides limited evidence about independently rejecting an excessively
broad, ambiguous product request. Answers, sources and citation mappings are fixtures.

### T3: a local interaction becoming a collection

Each first task kept prepared content, save outcome, front/back motion, interruption
and reduced motion within one responsibility. The continuation added multiple note
identities to the same card system. Per-note drafts, pending save ownership and
collection return were handled together.

A and C used a document View Transition to connect collection and editor geometry.
B used a shorter opacity reveal with stable numbering, selected-card indication,
scroll and focus restoration. More elaborate animation is not automatically better.

C's first delivery disclosed a projecting card edge crossing the small FIELDNOTE
index during a reverse turn. Its later record retained this concern, changed
perspective and recorded new samples. The maintainer viewed the original overlap
and a later clear sample; the angles and directions differ, so those images do not
prove every reversal is resolved. This is a concrete example of carrying an existing
quality responsibility into later work. A and B also made substantive visual repairs;
that behavior is not unique to C.

### What the product review added

**T1 — error priority across a shared visual-system change.** All conditions passed
the common notebook task assertions. Under the additional combination of a failed
note write and changing density, A and B replaced the note-save warning with a
preference-only message and hid Retry. The draft remained. C retained the original
save warning and Retry through density, theme and language changes. B's density
select also lacked a visible keyboard-focus cue; A and C showed one. C kept body
text at 16 px in both densities, while A and B used 15 px in Compact. These are
concrete tradeoffs and continuity differences, not a universal typography ranking.
C's more conspicuous error banner also displaced about 74 px of narrow reading
space in the observed fault state.

**T2 — neither coupled scope nor a polished panel ensures complete control.** C
held a deliberate 40 px upward wheel gesture while more output arrived; A and B
returned to following on the next output tick. All three held the larger upward
gesture. C nevertheless lacked a Stop action inside its narrow source modal, so
the person had to leave the source to stop. Its desktop close control also scrolled
outside the source viewport. A supplied source transport, but narrow Show paragraph
returned to the original citation after briefly measuring the requested target:
the target settled below the reader. B supplied source transport, but Stop → Try
again → Back → Latest could show reserved blank answer space during restart, still
blank in the later narrow checkpoint. Drafts remained intact in these cases.
The original reports give exact sequences, measurements, timing and uncertainties.

C exposes no counterpart to A/B's long-answer selector; the reviewer used its
fixed response, which overflowed at both sizes. Answer lengths therefore are not
equal workloads. The scroll differences cannot be attributed solely to the method.

**T3 — different collection/motion choices, with a remaining focus gap.** All three
completed the sampled creation, save, switching, failure, retry and reload paths.
B retained an introductory column, editable titles and a two-column collection;
A and C used wider three-column collections with visible shared-card movement.
On failed keyboard Save, A and C left focus on BODY. Retry remained reachable by
Tab, and the draft and prior stored data were retained. B kept Save focused. This
is a bounded continuity gap in C as well as A; the initial failure paths were not
fully reviewed, so it cannot be assigned to the continuation's origin. Clear-action
scope and confirmation also differed, as recorded in the original report.

These findings limit any broad reading of the authors' earlier passing checks.
The frozen experimental apps have not been repaired after review to manufacture
a better comparative result. Their remaining gaps belong to the affected product
responsibilities. C has useful observed strengths and real remaining weaknesses;
the study does not support a blanket superiority claim.

## Limits and release meaning

There is one independent sample per condition/family, three families and one
continuation per product. The model is stochastic; these outputs do not estimate
variance or isolate every instruction effect. B/C compares an integrated mechanism
that also changes optional templates and a neutral inspector. Fixed tasks,
available references and common modern-Web guidance constrain generalization.
Shared host load and recording overhead can affect timing. No statistical winner
or causal percentage improvement is claimed.

Reviewers are Agents, not human participants or the owner, and can miss defects.
Screenshots, ordered samples, recordings that merely exist, actually viewed media,
engineering assertions and user acceptance are different evidence. The study does
not establish Safari/Firefox behavior, physical mobile keyboards, assistive-technology
acceptance, cross-tab conflict handling or hardware frame pacing. Browser-local demos
retain their stated data limitations.

The release adds a compatible way to organize existing professional methods and
provides real applications exercising it. Formal declarations, complete records and
successful test suites do not certify design maturity. Further evaluations should
include repeated runs, an independently scoped broad brief, longer continuations
and human review under comparable total execution conditions. Those remain research
needs, not findings already established here.
