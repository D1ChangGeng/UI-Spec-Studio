# UI Spec Studio 1.17.0 — adaptive scope and continuous product design

Develop meaningful design increments at the scope their actual quality needs.
Preserve the full product goal while research, mature resources and implementation
evidence refine each formal UI SPEC's boundary.

- Define a formal SPEC by a cohesive experience goal, necessary relationships and
  an actually reviewable result in the evolving product.
- Use minimum sufficient experience closure to keep, split, merge or redraw scope.
  Init presents a revisable candidate structure; iterate can deepen the existing
  SPEC or start a distinct increment when its design purpose warrants one.
- Inherit, extend, adapt and revisit the same product. Keep internal searches,
  trials and repairs within their responsible SPEC and preserve open quality gaps.
- Connect target quality to viewed references, actual adopted implementations,
  rendered results and remaining differences. Verify the increment and its effects
  on the existing product at their respective scopes.
- Deliver useful decisions, comparisons and actual results in conversation.
  Product interfaces continue to serve their users' tasks.
- Optionally describe product context, candidate SPECs and formal SPECs in the
  existing evidence map. The inspector reports literal declarations and references;
  it does not plan, score, approve or certify the work.

The existing eight common references, optional shape, audit and finish, source
continuity, installation checks and release gates remain in place.

The registered three-condition study completed nine independent Agent runs and
eighteen task stages, followed by three anonymous product reviews. The results
include observable strengths, remaining interaction gaps and original failures.
They do not establish generalized visual superiority or an efficiency gain.
The released runtime is byte-identical to the study's integrated condition.
