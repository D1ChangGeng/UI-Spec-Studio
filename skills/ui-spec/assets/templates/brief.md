# UI SPEC project brief

Use the portions that clarify this task, or equivalent existing notes. This is an
optional aid; confirmed facts, useful questions and next actions matter more than fields.

## Product and people

Purpose, audience, use context, primary tasks and observable success.

## Intended experience and evidence questions

What the current user explicitly expects; what relevant audience evidence suggests;
what the reference product actually shows. Expand named products into task,
information, visual and temporal relationships, with the pertinent version/platform.
Separate confirmed goals, supported conventions, provisional hypotheses and gaps.

Map consequential parts, states, journeys and their relationships to the textual,
visual or behavioral evidence needed. State what discovery could change the next
choice. Allocate research by impact, uncertainty and plausible improvement; carry
the evolving experience model in the existing design record.

## Current project

Repository/frontend, existing components, run/test commands, actual product maturity,
source of product facts and authorized change scope.

## Complete goal and candidate increments

Retain the complete intended product experience, important functions, visual quality
and artistic commitments. Identify the current product UI and reusable foundations.
Outline candidate formal SPECs only as far as current evidence supports: coherent
experience goals, necessary relationships, likely dependencies and the first useful
scope. Keep this plan revisable as research, resource discovery and trials change
understanding. Explain the initial scope and its reason to the user.

## Accepted direction

Visual identity, typography/language coverage, palette roles, density, composition,
spacing, imagery, motion and links to the existing design-system document.

## Page families and journeys

Responsibility, shared shell, entry, next actions, relevant roles and recovery.
Keep detailed coverage in the manifest or the project's existing equivalent.

## Component decisions

Role | source/component | checked URL/version/date | selection reason | adaptation/license

## Verification scope

Viewports, supported variants, critical states, representative data, browser access,
privacy of evidence and the reason for any scoped exclusion.

## Direction and open decisions

Binding user preferences, agent-selected reversible assumptions, material unresolved
facts and the smallest next observation that could change the design.

## Current round

Selected outcome, why now, accepted assumptions, implemented result, review status,
approval reference and next meaningful action.

For formal design work, identify the active SPEC's experience goal, quality
responsibility and necessary connected objects. Distinguish research/observation,
primary implementation and integration/regression scopes. Note what is inherited,
extended, adapted or reconsidered in the current product. Keep internal research,
repairs and refinement under this SPEC while they serve its promised goal.

## Scope continuity

Identify implemented, inspected, approved, connected and verified scopes separately.
Record the source of approval and what the next round should inherit. The active
operation describes current work; individual scopes can have different maturity.

When a boundary changes, preserve the complete goal, why the change is justified,
where the responsibility now sits and the original unresolved findings. A new
SPEC or a narrower current scope does not itself resolve a quality gap. Keep
the actual preview, important choices and observed results visible in the
conversation alongside the full project record.

## User-level product model

First action, working object, continuing loop, result location, mature flow
references and primary/secondary model. State observed facts and provisional
assumptions. Link the design decision/resource map and its direction authority.

## Reuse scope

Preserved business/engineering assets, accepted design constraints, and current
visual candidates assessed against the new quality target.

## Content and language scope

User-facing content owner, core terminology, supported languages, product Voice and situational Tone, key consequential conditions, and important state copy. Link established content/style guidance or the relevant content decision record.

## Runtime and maintenance context

Relevant repeated tasks, readiness and completion points, device/network conditions,
content growth, update roles/frequency, shared sources and meaningful overrides.
Record the consequential uncertainty and planned observation in the existing brief.
