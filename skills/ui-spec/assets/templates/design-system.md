# Design system

## Direction and intended experience

Describe task requirements, explicit aesthetic goals, the chosen theme and source
roles. Identify familiar experience relationships and deliberate expressive changes.
State product, demonstration or authored-output scope and selection authority.

## Shared roles

Typography; foreground/surface/emphasis/status colors; spacing relationships; radii;
borders/elevation; focus; icons; image treatment; motion and reduced-motion equivalents.
Define focal/supporting roles, purposeful differences and applicable exceptions;
connect them to tokens/variables and trials with populated work and long content.

## Page families

Shared anchors, content origin, sizing limits, density, growth, scroll ownership and
short-height/narrow-screen behavior for each family.

## Components and states

Reusable behavior, selected sources, input/error/empty/loading/disabled states,
keyboard/touch expectations and state-preservation rules.

## Variants

Supported languages, themes, preference initialization/persistence, long content,
fonts, RTL where applicable and native-control treatment.

## Acceptance reference

Approved scope, revision, matching fixtures, screenshot/test references and approved
intentional differences. Separate experience validity and expressive completion.
For fidelity work, identify the selected/approved source and details to retain.
Keep current decisions here; Git records prior revisions.

## Visual asset language and delivery

Identity/functional icon roles, type/glyph intent, image crop/lighting/material,
spatial and motion relationships. Link significant masters and variants to the
asset records; preserve their source, rights, runtime/fallback and acceptance.
Evaluate each asset on its own and in the shared composition.
