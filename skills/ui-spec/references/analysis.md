# Analysis: decide what is worth building

## Nine dimensions, one coherent experience

Use these dimensions together to understand the current increment. User outcomes, authoritative product facts, inclusive use and observable evidence apply across them. Give depth to the questions that can change the outcome; a concise decision with sound reasons is sufficient. The dimensions identify design objects, while the eight common references organize the professional methods.

| Dimension | What to establish | Effect on the work |
|---|---|---|
| Product and interaction model | People, primary objects, recognizable task loop, real choices, roles and outcomes | Choose a meaningful surface and its operating relationships |
| Visual design and art direction | Hierarchy, character, material, composition and craft target | Establish a coherent direction and compare mature foundations |
| Visual assets and asset engineering | Identity, type, imagery, spatial/dynamic material, production and delivery | Let suitable assets shape the concept and reach the final placement |
| Content design and typography | Needed understanding, factual authority, voice, placement, language and text weight | Compose real messages with the reading and action path |
| Layout and spatial composition | Proportions, stable anchors, responsive behavior, motion envelope and content growth | Allocate space and choose scroll ownership by task |
| Components and interaction states | Semantics, actual choice space, materials, input methods and complete states | Preserve understandable and accessible behavior |
| Motion and interaction continuity | Object identity, transitions, timing, interruption and feedback | Express real changes while retaining orientation and control |
| Performance and experience engineering | Input-to-result path, readiness, scheduling, loading, resource cost and recovery | Improve repeated work and verify gains under stated conditions |
| Operational UX and sustainable content | Content sources, editing roles, templates/overrides, bulk work and lifecycle | Keep routine maintenance efficient and published results reliable |

Content can reshape navigation; a key asset can alter composition; realistic latency can alter preparation and feedback; frequent updates can require reusable content or a bulk operation. Follow those relationships to the responsible method. [Content](content-design.md), [visual principles](principles.md), [assets](visual-assets.md), [interaction](interaction-and-variants.md), [verification](verification.md) and real [integration](integration.md) supply complementary decisions rather than separate pass scores.

## Use theory to investigate real design work

Use design theory to assess concrete mature work: hierarchy, rhythm, weight, interaction, implementation and fit. Search while the representation is open, compare connected decisions, implement and observe. A useful discovery can improve the initial idea. For a new surface, investigate a coherent page, system or block before local parts; for a bounded correction, inherit the accepted foundation and focus on the affected relationship. Original work fills product-specific gaps and coordinates the result.

## Recognize the product through its use

Start with the user's first meaningful action, primary working object, repeated action/result loop, session continuity and durable outcome. Observe actual flows or supplied evidence, and use source contracts to recover capabilities. Keep observation, inference and unresolved intent distinguishable. A screenshot supports a composition observation; runtime behavior and research activity require their own evidence.

Describe the proposed experience in plain language: “people ask a question, examine an answer with sources, then continue the same thread,” or “people open a record, edit its properties and return to a filtered collection.” This working model is more useful than an internal label such as platform, desk or workspace.

| Model candidate | Strong user-level signals | Consequences to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Conversational assistant | Intent begins in a composer; responses lead to follow-ups | New and resumed threads, readable conversation, persistent input, generation/stop/retry and sources/artifacts |
| Document / information space | People create, organize, edit and revisit durable objects | Hierarchy or collection views, content editing, search, linking and object continuity |
| Task / operational system | People prioritize, assign, advance and inspect work items | Lists/boards, status, filters, detail, progress and efficient repeated operations |
| Canvas / editor | People manipulate an artifact directly | Selection, tools, contextual properties, undo and spatial continuity |
| Discovery / decision flow | People search, compare and commit to a choice | Scanable collections, comparison, detail, consequence and recovery |
| Reading / publication | People follow an argument or locate an answer | Narrative order, headings, references and an appropriate next action |

Use these as hypotheses, including other models the task warrants. A hybrid names its primary loop and explains when a secondary surface appears. For example, a conversation may open an evidence document alongside the thread. Each pane has a task and state owner. Product type, local page shape and visual language are separate decisions: a dense operational view and an empty assistant start screen should express their distinct jobs even within one brand.

Inspect a relevant mature product's comparable flow: starting point, first action, navigation, result, continuation and recovery. Identify transferable conventions, actual source/date and observed states. Use official explanations for documented behavior; use live interaction or viewable examples for claims about motion or appearance. Describe current access limitations precisely. Product names orient discussion; the record explains which specific convention is adopted and why it fits. Keep original branding, content and distinctive implementation separate from those conventions.

## Establish the reuse boundary for this assignment

Read the requested change as a local improvement, visual refresh, interaction redesign or combined redesign. Infer the scope from the brief and confirm only a material ambiguity. Preserve working business rules, data contracts, permissions and necessary capabilities. Evaluate layout, navigation, components, typography, palette and motion against the new target, alongside external candidates.

Existing source is evidence of what works and what can be adapted. Explicit user acceptance is evidence of approved design. Link inherited visual decisions to that acceptance or to a current fit assessment. Preserve valuable internal UI when it meets the target; improve or replace its presentation layer when another solution better serves the intended experience. Keep framework and architecture changes proportional to the work.

## Confirm consequential direction with useful options

Check whether credible alternatives would change the primary action, information architecture, enduring working object or overall visual identity. When those choices remain unresolved and reshape substantial work, present a small set of distinct directions, a recommendation and the practical tradeoff before expanding the shell or page family. Two or three options are often enough; one well-supported direction can be presented with its rationale.

Make a comparison concrete: the same representative task and content, the first action, organization of results, continuation, relevant mature references and a small preview when spatial differences matter. Hold incidental styling steady when comparing interaction models; compare themes separately when that is the open choice. Give the user a focused choice about the experience they intend, while the agent owns component and implementation details.

Established intent, an approved design or explicit delegated autonomy supports direct implementation. In one-shot/no-follow-up work, choose the best-supported direction and label its assumptions, unresolved alternatives and pending owner acceptance. A local prototype selection is a preference for that prototype; formal design approval retains its actual source and scope. Continue independent work while awaiting a significant choice.

## Lead users with different levels of direction

For a goal-only request, infer likely tasks and interaction models, research comparable flows and recommend a reviewable direction. Use the consequential-direction check above for material open choices. Supplied references may express exact implementation, aesthetic qualities or product constraints; identify which, preserve binding preferences and fill the remaining gaps. Existing decisions continue across rounds. Resolve routine details independently and ask only when an unrecoverable fact determines a consequential commitment, while advancing unaffected work.

## Reason about uncertainty

Product uncertainty calls for task/content analysis. Aesthetic uncertainty often benefits from a small rendered composition. Technical uncertainty benefits from an isolated implementation probe. Money, permissions, retention, publication and other consequential business commitments require an authoritative rule or explicit authorization.

Choose the next action using impact, reversibility, current evidence and cost of obtaining better evidence. A low-risk uncertainty often supports an agent-made assumption and a visible trial. A confident preference still benefits from rendering and interaction checks. Keep room for exploration where a decision is cheap to revise; establish important constraints early where they can invalidate later work.

A design direction should be specific enough to guide choices: audience/task, desired tone, content hierarchy, typographic character, density, imagery and motion. Tie the explanation to the subject. Keep factual claims and testimonials anchored in real supplied material; use identifiable representative fixtures to test layout.

## Define each surface by responsibility

Establish its audience/role, job, main action, relevant content and states, entry, next action, shared shell and data owner. Choose the appropriate presentation: public introduction, reading surface, collection/detail, focused task, workspace or overlay.

Merge surfaces when their user task, authority and lifecycle are the same and the difference can be represented by entry context or state. Keep distinct responsibilities separate even when their layouts look alike. Preserve meaningful URLs, browser back/forward, discoverable navigation and task-local actions.

Describe a task as a directed state graph or a short transition table. Return, retry, cancellation and recovery are ordinary parts of the graph. For a meaningful transition, identify its visible control or legitimate entry, precondition, destination, retained context and result. A preview selector establishes display access; actual controls establish task continuity.

For example, a sign-in interruption can preserve an editing draft and return target, establish a session, then resume the intended save with server validation. The page responsibilities follow that task rather than the number of screens a template provides.

## Choose a bounded increment

A strong round improves an identifiable outcome, runs independently enough to inspect, and is small enough for focused feedback. It may be one page, a connected journey, a shared component family or a systemic correction.

Prioritize task/correctness/accessibility blockers, shared defects, missing valuable journeys, design-affecting integration risks, then supporting states and refinement. Adjust that order for explicit goals and actual impact. Consider reach, dependency order, effort and what the round will teach. Explain the important tradeoff briefly; a mandatory scoring formula adds little to a clear choice.

When told to continue, select the next increment from the accepted scope, incomplete journeys and observed defects. A user can redirect that choice at any point. Keep new exploration proportional to what remains unresolved.

## Preserve the right facts

User intent explains the desired outcome; approved design describes accepted experience; code and runtime describe present capability; tests describe observed behavior. Record tensions between these sources rather than treating one as an answer to every question.

For development builds, evolve current code and data models with reproducible initialization. For operating products, derive compatibility, data and availability work from actual obligations. Use the current framework and meaningful abstractions. Scope architecture changes to what the desired experience truly requires.

Record reusable decisions where the project already keeps them. Keep source links and the smallest rationale needed for another round to make the right choice. This enables continuity while leaving detailed implementation in code and history in Git.

## Make content decisions with the product model

Establish who owns the facts and what users need to comprehend at each step. Analyze text as a design object: names, labels, guidance, status, error and consequential conditions can determine whether a page or component is useful. Research mature product copy and vocabulary alongside layout and resource candidates. Preserve the difference between a useful wording convention and another product's actual claims. Align significant navigation or commitment language with the product owner. Apply the [common content method](content-design.md) when turning these decisions into actual copy and typography.

## Design the understanding before the arrangement

For the current task, identify what the person knows on arrival, what they must understand next, what evidence supports that understanding and which action becomes reasonable afterward. Use this to order and group content. A reachable purchase button establishes access; clear differences, obligations and credible evidence establish an informed choice.

Give each meaningful region a communication job: orient, explain, compare, substantiate, decide, act or recover. Several jobs may share a region. Choose copy, examples, comparisons, imagery, diagrams or direct manipulation by how well they answer the user's question. A README often needs fit, a working installation command, a usage example and a way to inspect evidence before its full architecture. A workspace may expose the next action alongside the evidence needed to take it.

Write with representative real content early. Headings state meaning; labels name objects consistently; actions describe their consequences; feedback explains actual state and recovery. Distinguish facts, hypotheses, illustrative values and promises. Respect user-authored content and factual copy when improving expression. Treat length, terminology, units and localization as part of the design material.

## Choose a representation before a component

Match the user's cognitive operation to a useful form. These are candidate relationships, not automatic prescriptions:

| Needed understanding or operation | Candidate representation | Check before choosing |
|---|---|---|
| Compare the same properties across objects | Aligned table, comparison rows or coordinated small multiples | Comparable units, consistent order, missing values and narrow-screen reading |
| Find one item in a repeated collection | List/grid with meaningful filters and selection | Distinguishing attributes, scan density and retained query context |
| Understand a dependency or state transition | Diagram, timeline or explicit state presentation | Correct direction, guards, cycles and a readable alternative |
| Inspect the basis for a claim | Claim/evidence pairing, traceable citation or coordinated panes | Provenance, visible association, permissions and navigation back |
| See how a parameter changes a result | Direct manipulation with timely feedback | Safe bounds, actual authority, latency and reversible changes |
| Learn a sequence | Ordered steps, worked example or progressive disclosure | Prerequisites, exceptions, recovery and whether order actually matters |

Choose a representation that preserves the important relationship, and test it against concrete mature implementations. A strong candidate can refine that choice. Select the appropriate page, block, component or production technique at the useful reuse level. A graph's visual attraction is useful only when its topology helps the task. Distinguish categories, quantities, sequence and uncertainty in data graphics; show units, scale, baseline and missingness honestly. Simulated charts are labeled as fixtures.

## Organize actions around the object and real choices

Identify the object the person believes they are acting on and keep its information, controls and feedback visibly associated. A card can hold detail and a state change when that fits the task; a list, table or conversation can provide a clearer carrier for another product. Preserve a stable identity when the object moves, expands, flips or changes state, including the return path.

Inventory decisions the user actually has to make. Multiple valid configurations need a usable choice; a single valid configuration usually needs a clear value; a choice already made upstream needs an understandable summary and an appropriate way to revise it. Keep necessary control and important conditions visible. Determine whether actions are primary/secondary, equal alternatives, sequential steps or consequential commitments before assigning visual weight. Two checkout actions being equal in one flow is evidence for that flow, not a default button rule.

## Understand the operating life of the design

Identify who supplies and maintains content, how often it changes, the volume of repeated work and which variants need individual treatment. A shared template can remove repeated editing while a deliberate override preserves object-specific differences. Make the effective content, its source and the impact of a shared change understandable. Choose batch controls from real workload, selection scope and recovery needs rather than adding an administration layer to every product.

Describe each role's task using the same authoritative facts. A consumer may need service availability and a next action; an operator may need affected objects, diagnostic context and a repair path. Derive useful information and control from that role's actual permission. Let maintenance needs affect the content model and interface early; use the content and integration references for ownership, publication, conflict and partial-result details.

## Form a concept that carries across surfaces

Derive an organizing idea from the product's mechanism, audience, subject and available material. “Evidence beside conclusions” can guide reading panes, citations and source selection; “premium” needs those concrete relationships. Separate inherited identity, surface composition and local refinement. Use accepted choices as constraints, then compare materially different organizations when the choice can improve the task.

Evaluate product fit, clarity, distinctiveness, extension to related surfaces, feasibility and user control. Precise density and excellent status handling can distinguish a tool as effectively as expressive art distinguishes a gallery. Keep controls recognizable and accessible. A concept earns its place through mutually supporting decisions.

## Read references as mechanisms

Select references for a named quality: a comparison's scanability, a publication's type hierarchy, a transition's orientation, or a visual identity's discipline. Use whole → relationships → details observation. Describe what is visible first, then the mechanism you infer. Record uncertain claims as hypotheses; observe live behavior when motion or interaction matters.

Identify what carries the reference's quality, what relies on its content/assets/platform, and what transfers to this task. A photographic composition depends on suitable imagery; its grid alone may carry little of its effect. Learn the grammar while producing original composition and respecting assets' rights. Product popularity and an author's preferred palette supply context rather than universal rules.

## Make decisions explainable and adjustable

Keep significant choices traceable through intended outcome, selected relationship, implementation, tradeoff and confirming observation. A paragraph in existing records can suffice. Expose meaningful controls in source: semantic tokens, content fields, component states, composition bounds, image focal points and timing. Preserve their purpose across later changes. Routine adjustments stay in code; successful examples retain their applicable conditions.

## Let available excellence inform the solution

Translate the desired perceptual outcome and operation into concrete resource candidates while the relevant decision remains open. Compare useful internal, native and external work by clarity, task behavior, quality and total cost. Preserve binding direction and record why the selected scope fits. The [sourcing reference](component-sourcing.md) owns discovery and adoption methods; the catalog supplies leads; project evidence establishes the result.

## Let assets participate in the concept

Identify which material carries identity, content, spatial meaning or attention, then inspect it while the concept and layout can still change. Distinguish routine functional icons, accurate third-party marks and original product identity. Type selection, custom lettering, illustration and 3D have different quality and delivery needs; apply the common [asset method](visual-assets.md).

Compare significant candidates in the intended context, including actual small sizes and themes when relevant. Extract contour, proportion, material and timing relationships from references, and develop an independent product expression. Let users confirm consequential identity choices through equivalent previews, while the agent handles normal production decisions. Asset availability, permissions, tool access and output quality can each change the viable direction.
