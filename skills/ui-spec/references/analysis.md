## Establish the product's target experience

Use **Product UI Experience Alignment** to connect tasks and aesthetic goals to mature evidence and verified implementation. Establish readable hierarchy, component craft, complete states and continuous use through adopted resources, with independent product expression. Judge specific qualities rather than resemblance percentages.

Separate required behavior/accessibility/business conditions, explicit aesthetic requirements, and revisable visual conventions. Artistic impact or differentiation requested by the user remains an acceptance requirement in professional software too. Task familiarity concerns understandable objects, actions and results; navigation geometry, component shape and material can change when the new cues work. Resolve concrete conflicts through trials before revising the agreed direction.

Treat a product name as a compressed experience description. Establish whether it refers to a public website, working application, flow, brand character or particular relationship. “Like a conversational AI product” could concern task entry, attachments, history, long-answer reading or a companion workspace. Turn the relevant interpretation into questions about space, actions, content, feedback and continuity. Show researched options when interpretations materially change direction; infer and investigate routine details.

Without a named reference, research the task and working objects. Preserve proven semantics and suitable assets in upgrades; verify new combinations of mature patterns for novel interactions. Sources may address task, region, state, asset or time. Coordinate them through a leading visual language and judge fit in context; reputation alone is insufficient.

Use these dimensions together to understand the current increment. User outcomes, authoritative product facts, inclusive use and observable evidence apply across them. ## Use theory to investigate real design work

Turn intent into questions that can change a choice. Search by task, pattern, component role, expression and implementation need; revise questions from actual browsing. New surfaces benefit from coherent foundations; bounded corrections can inherit accepted work.

A bright panel may outweigh the task; retained selection may preserve orientation. Test these in context. Keep reference, original demo, local trial and journey evidence distinct; a trial can reopen selection. [Verification](verification.md) governs conditions and claim limits.

## Calibrate exploration and design confidence

For each consequential decision, consider three qualitative variables: **impact** on product perception or use, **uncertainty** about what mature work offers, and **potential improvement** from further discovery. Ask whether the next useful evidence comes from broader research, a deeper perceptual reading, a missing state, a distinct alternative or a local trial. Investigate small but defining assets with the same seriousness as dominant page structures when their design leverage warrants it.

Confidence belongs to a specific claim supported by evidence, not to an arbitrary percentage or agent familiarity. Ask what was **actually viewed**, which conditions were covered, how reliable and relevant the source is, whether alternatives differ in substance and whether the chosen resource survives a product-context trial. Many similar candidates do not establish breadth; a beautiful promotional cut does not establish continuous operation.

Look for a credible counterexample: which different composition, type, asset, interaction or motion might change this choice? Follow cross-domain findings even if they challenge the initial product-style hypothesis. Converge when consequential questions are answered well enough for implementation and continued search has lower expected value than testing or delivery; carry any material unobserved condition forward without imagining its result.

## Recognize the product through its use

Start with the first meaningful action, primary working object, repeated action/result loop, session continuity and durable outcome. Observe actual flows or supplied evidence; read source contracts for capabilities. Distinguish observation, inference and unresolved intent.

Describe the experience in user terms: “ask a question, examine a sourced answer and continue the thread,” or “edit a record and return to the filtered collection.” Use that model to guide the interface.

| Model candidate | Strong user-level signals | Consequences to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Conversational assistant | Intent begins in a composer; responses lead to follow-ups | New/resumed threads, readable conversation, persistent input, generation/stop/retry and sources/artifacts |
| Document / information space | People create, organize, edit and revisit durable objects | Hierarchy/collections, editing, search, linking and object continuity |
| Task / operational system | People prioritize, assign, advance and inspect work items | Lists/boards, status, filters, detail, progress and repeated operations |
| Canvas / editor | People manipulate an artifact directly | Selection, tools, contextual properties, undo and spatial continuity |
| Discovery / decision flow | People search, compare and commit to a choice | Scannable collections, comparison, detail, consequence and recovery |
| Reading / publication | People follow an argument or locate an answer | Narrative order, headings, references and an appropriate next action |

Treat these as hypotheses and include other models when needed. A hybrid names its primary loop and the secondary surface's purpose: a conversation may open an evidence document beside the thread. Give each pane a task and state owner. Product model, local page shape and visual language are separate choices; a dense workspace and an empty assistant entry can share a brand while serving different jobs.

Inspect comparable starting points, actions, results, continuation and recovery. Retain the convention, source/date and states viewed. Documentation supports described behavior; actual views and operation support their observed appearance and motion. Preserve provenance and rights when transferring a convention.

## Confirm consequential direction with useful options

Check whether credible alternatives would change the primary action, information architecture, enduring working object or overall identity. When unresolved choices reshape substantial work, offer a small set of distinct directions, a recommendation and the practical tradeoff before extending the shell or page family. One well-supported direction may suffice.

Compare the same task and content at comparable completion. Hold incidental styling steady for interaction-model comparisons; for art direction, show material differences in assets, composition or motion. Use actual renders or runnable fragments, including populated core use. Color swaps or unequal polish cannot establish a fair direction comparison. The user resolves consequential open choices; routine implementation remains agent work.

Established intent, approval or delegated autonomy supports direct progress. One-shot work retains its assumptions, unresolved alternatives and pending owner acceptance. Prototype preference and formal approval keep their actual scope. Continue independent work while a significant choice is pending.

## Reason about uncertainty

Match the next observation to the uncertainty: task/content analysis for product questions, a rendered composition or sequence for aesthetic questions, and an isolated probe for implementation questions. Consequential business commitments require authoritative facts or authorization.

## Define each surface by responsibility

Establish audience/role, job, main action, content/states, entry, continuation, shared shell and data owner. Choose the appropriate introduction, reading surface, collection/detail, focused task, workspace or overlay.

Merge surfaces when task, authority and lifecycle are shared and entry context or state explains the difference. Keep distinct responsibilities separate, with meaningful URLs, back/forward behavior and task-local actions.

A state graph or short transition table can identify the visible control, precondition, destination, retained context and result. Include return, retry, cancellation and recovery. A preview selector exposes a state; actual controls establish task continuity. For example, sign-in can preserve a draft and return target, then resume the intended save under server validation.

## Choose a bounded increment

Choose an identifiable user outcome that can run, be inspected and be revised coherently: a page, connected journey, shared family or systemic repair. Prioritize task/accessibility blockers, shared defects, valuable missing paths and design-affecting integration, adjusted for explicit goals. Consider reach, dependencies, effort and what the round can establish.

“Continue” resumes from accepted scope, incomplete journeys and observed defects. State the next useful increment and its important tradeoff briefly. Keep new exploration proportional to unresolved questions.

## Design the understanding before the arrangement

Identify what people know on arrival, what they need next, the evidence supporting it and the action that becomes reasonable. Order and group content accordingly. Clear differences and obligations enable an informed choice alongside a reachable action.

Give each region a communication job: orient, explain, compare, substantiate, decide, act or recover. Choose copy, examples, imagery, diagrams or direct manipulation by the question answered. A README may need fit, installation and usage before architecture; a workspace may pair the next action with its evidence.

Compose representative content early. Headings state meaning, names stay consistent, actions describe consequences and feedback reflects actual state. Preserve factual and user-authored meaning while improving wording, units, length and localization. Distinguish facts, hypotheses, illustrative values and promises.

## Choose a representation before a component

Match the person's cognitive operation to a useful form; inspect mature examples before selecting an implementation.

| Needed understanding or operation | Candidate representation | Check before choosing |
|---|---|---|
| Compare properties across objects | Aligned table, comparison rows or coordinated small multiples | Comparable units/order, missing values and narrow-screen reading |
| Find an item in a collection | List/grid with filters and selection | Distinguishing attributes, density and retained query context |
| Understand dependencies or transitions | Diagram, timeline or explicit state presentation | Correct direction, guards, cycles and a readable alternative |
| Inspect the basis for a claim | Claim/evidence pairing, citation or coordinated panes | Provenance, visible association, permissions and return |
| See how a parameter changes a result | Direct manipulation with timely feedback | Safe bounds, authority, latency and reversibility |
| Learn a sequence | Ordered steps, worked example or progressive disclosure | Prerequisites, exceptions, recovery and actual ordering needs |

These are candidates, with fit established through content and use. A strong resource can refine the representation. A graph earns its complexity when topology aids the task. Distinguish categories, quantities, sequence and uncertainty; preserve units, scale, baseline and missingness. Label simulated charts as fixtures.
