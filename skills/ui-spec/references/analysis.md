## Nine dimensions, one coherent experience

Use these dimensions together to understand the current increment. User outcomes, authoritative product facts, inclusive use and observable evidence apply across them. Give depth to the questions that can change the outcome; a concise decision with sound reasons is sufficient. The dimensions identify design objects, while the eight common references organize the professional methods.

| Dimension | What to establish | Effect on the work |
|---|---|---|
| Product and interaction model | People, primary objects, recognizable task loop, real choices, roles and outcomes | Choose a meaningful surface and its operating relationships |
| Visual design and art direction | Hierarchy, character, material, composition and craft target | Establish a coherent direction and compare mature foundations |
| Visual assets and asset engineering | Identity, type, imagery, spatial/dynamic material, production and delivery | Let suitable assets shape the concept and reach the final placement |
| Content design and typography | Needed understanding, factual authority, voice, placement, language and text weight | Compose real messages with the reading and action path |
| Layout and spatial composition | Proportions, stable anchors, responsive behavior, motion envelope and content growth | Allocate space and choose scroll ownership by task |
| Components and interaction states | Semantics, actual choice space, materials, input methods and complete states | Preserve understandable and accessible behavior |
| Motion and interaction continuity | Object identity, transitions, timing, interruption and feedback | Express real changes while retaining orientation and control |
| Performance and experience engineering | Input-to-result path, readiness, scheduling, loading, resource cost and recovery | Improve repeated work and verify gains under stated conditions |
| Operational UX and sustainable content | Content sources, editing roles, templates/overrides, bulk work and lifecycle | Keep routine maintenance efficient and published results reliable |

Content can reshape navigation; a key asset can alter composition; realistic latency can alter preparation and feedback; frequent updates can require reusable content or a bulk operation. Follow those relationships to the responsible method. [Content](content-design.md), [visual principles](principles.md), [assets](visual-assets.md), [interaction](interaction-and-variants.md), [verification](verification.md) and real [integration](integration.md) supply complementary decisions rather than separate pass scores.

## Use theory to investigate real design work

Use design theory while searching, viewing, comparing, adapting and rechecking concrete work. For a new surface, investigate a coherent page, system or block before local parts; a bounded correction can inherit the accepted foundation. Let a strong visual discovery revise the initial idea. Original work fills product-specific gaps and coordinates the result.

Connect **observed phenomenon → perceptual effect → causal hypothesis → design choice → rendered recheck**. Describe the visible relationship and its viewing condition, then explain how it may affect attention, grouping, reading or object recognition. Inspect implementation where it can test the cause. A reference may keep its selected row visible as details expand, suggesting an anchor worth adopting. A bright side panel may dominate the intended working area because its area and contrast outweigh the primary content. Compare the proposed adoption or repair in the complete frame and relevant sequence. Keep professional judgment and observed user response distinguishable.

Choose an observation unit that contains the behavior being judged: a rendered frame, a time-related sequence or a connected journey. Inspect source-resource quality, the combined composition and actual runtime results separately. A selected asset or component can change the layout it joins; an integration finding can reopen the selection. The [verification method](verification.md) governs capture conditions, temporal evidence and honest inspection claims.

## Recognize the product through its use

Start with the first meaningful action, primary working object, repeated action/result loop, session continuity and durable outcome. Observe actual flows or supplied evidence; read source contracts for capabilities. Distinguish observation, inference and unresolved intent.

Describe the experience in user terms: “ask a question, examine a sourced answer and continue the thread,” or “edit a record and return to the filtered collection.” Use that model to guide the interface.

| Model candidate | Strong user-level signals | Consequences to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Conversational assistant | Intent begins in a composer; responses lead to follow-ups | New/resumed threads, readable conversation, persistent input, generation/stop/retry and sources/artifacts |
| Document / information space | People create, organize, edit and revisit durable objects | Hierarchy/collections, editing, search, linking and object continuity |
| Task / operational system | People prioritize, assign, advance and inspect work items | Lists/boards, status, filters, detail, progress and repeated operations |
| Canvas / editor | People manipulate an artifact directly | Selection, tools, contextual properties, undo and spatial continuity |
| Discovery / decision flow | People search, compare and commit to a choice | Scannable collections, comparison, detail, consequence and recovery |
| Reading / publication | People follow an argument or locate an answer | Narrative order, headings, references and an appropriate next action |

Treat these as hypotheses and include other models when needed. A hybrid names its primary loop and the secondary surface's purpose: a conversation may open an evidence document beside the thread. Give each pane a task and state owner. Product model, local page shape and visual language are separate choices; a dense workspace and an empty assistant entry can share a brand while serving different jobs.

Inspect a mature product's comparable starting point, action, result, continuation and recovery. Record the specific convention, source/date and states actually viewed. Official explanations support documented behavior; visible examples and live operation support appearance and motion judgments. Preserve source identity and rights when transferring a convention.

## Confirm consequential direction with useful options

Check whether credible alternatives would change the primary action, information architecture, enduring working object or overall identity. When unresolved choices reshape substantial work, offer a small set of distinct directions, a recommendation and the practical tradeoff before extending the shell or page family. One well-supported direction may suffice.

Compare the same task and representative content: first action, result organization, continuation and relevant mature examples. Show a small rendered probe when spatial or temporal differences affect the decision. Hold incidental styling steady when comparing interaction models; compare themes separately when that is the question. The user chooses consequential direction; the agent handles routine implementation.

Established intent, approval or delegated autonomy supports direct progress. One-shot work retains its assumptions, unresolved alternatives and pending owner acceptance. Prototype preference and formal approval keep their actual scope. Continue independent work while a significant choice is pending.

## Reason about uncertainty

Match the next observation to the uncertainty: task/content analysis for product questions, a rendered composition or sequence for aesthetic questions, and an isolated probe for implementation questions. Consequential business commitments require authoritative facts or authorization.

Choose by impact, reversibility, current evidence and the cost of learning more. Test reversible assumptions in a visible trial; establish constraints early when they can invalidate substantial work. Make direction concrete through audience, task, content hierarchy, type, density, imagery and motion. Use real supplied facts for promises and identifiable fixtures for exploration.

## Define each surface by responsibility

Establish audience/role, job, main action, content/states, entry, continuation, shared shell and data owner. Choose the appropriate introduction, reading surface, collection/detail, focused task, workspace or overlay.

Merge surfaces when task, authority and lifecycle are shared and entry context or state explains the difference. Keep distinct responsibilities separate, with meaningful URLs, back/forward behavior and task-local actions.

A state graph or short transition table can identify the visible control, precondition, destination, retained context and result. Include return, retry, cancellation and recovery. A preview selector exposes a state; actual controls establish task continuity. For example, sign-in can preserve a draft and return target, then resume the intended save under server validation.

## Choose a bounded increment

Choose an identifiable user outcome that can run, be inspected and be revised coherently: a page, connected journey, shared family or systemic repair. Prioritize task/accessibility blockers, shared defects, valuable missing paths and design-affecting integration, adjusted for explicit goals. Consider reach, dependencies, effort and what the round can establish.

“Continue” resumes from accepted scope, incomplete journeys and observed defects. State the next useful increment and its important tradeoff briefly. Keep new exploration proportional to unresolved questions.

## Preserve the right facts

Use intent for the desired outcome, approval for accepted design, source/runtime for current capability and tests for their observed behavior. Resolve tensions at the responsible layer.

Development builds can evolve reproducible initialization and current models; operating products retain actual data and availability obligations. Keep architecture proportional to the change. Store consequential decisions, sources and a useful rationale in existing project records; source and Git retain implementation detail and history.

## Design the understanding before the arrangement

Identify what people know on arrival, what they need next, the evidence supporting it and the action that becomes reasonable. Order and group content accordingly. Clear differences and obligations enable an informed choice alongside a reachable action.

Give each region a communication job: orient, explain, compare, substantiate, decide, act or recover. Choose copy, examples, imagery, diagrams or direct manipulation by the question answered. A README may need fit, installation and usage before architecture; a workspace may pair the next action with its evidence.

Compose representative content early. Headings state meaning, names stay consistent, actions describe consequences and feedback reflects actual state. Preserve factual and user-authored meaning while improving wording, units, length and localization. Distinguish facts, hypotheses, illustrative values and promises.

## Choose a representation before a component

Match the person's cognitive operation to a useful form; inspect mature examples before selecting an implementation.

| Needed understanding or operation | Candidate representation | Check before choosing |
|---|---|---|
| Compare properties across objects | Aligned table, comparison rows or coordinated small multiples | Comparable units/order, missing values and narrow-screen reading |
| Find an item in a collection | List/grid with filters and selection | Distinguishing attributes, density and retained query context |
| Understand dependencies or transitions | Diagram, timeline or explicit state presentation | Correct direction, guards, cycles and a readable alternative |
| Inspect the basis for a claim | Claim/evidence pairing, citation or coordinated panes | Provenance, visible association, permissions and return |
| See how a parameter changes a result | Direct manipulation with timely feedback | Safe bounds, authority, latency and reversibility |
| Learn a sequence | Ordered steps, worked example or progressive disclosure | Prerequisites, exceptions, recovery and actual ordering needs |

These are candidates, with fit established through content and use. A strong resource can refine the representation. A graph earns its complexity when topology aids the task. Distinguish categories, quantities, sequence and uncertainty; preserve units, scale, baseline and missingness. Label simulated charts as fixtures.

## Organize actions around the object and real choices

Keep the recognized object, its information, actions and feedback visibly associated. Select a card, row, table, message or editor from its actual use. Preserve identity as it moves, expands or changes state, including the return path.

Inventory real decisions: alternatives need a usable choice; one valid value needs clear presentation; an upstream choice needs a summary and a permitted way to revise it. Keep necessary conditions visible. Establish primary/secondary, peer, sequential or consequential action relationships before assigning visual weight. Equal checkout buttons can fit one flow without defining other products.

## Understand the operating life of the design

Identify content owners, update frequency, repeated workload and individual differences. Use shared templates and deliberate overrides where they reduce work, and make effective content, inheritance and change impact understandable. Derive batch controls from actual scale, selection and recovery needs.

Express the same authoritative condition for each role's task and permission. A consumer may need availability and a next action; an operator may need causes, affected objects and repair controls. Let these needs shape the model and interface early; use the content and integration references for publication, conflict and partial-result details.

## Form a concept that carries across surfaces

Derive an organizing idea from mechanism, audience, subject and available material. “Evidence beside conclusions” can guide panes, citations and selection; an aesthetic adjective needs concrete relationships. Separate inherited identity, surface composition and local refinement. Use accepted choices as constraints and compare alternatives when they can improve the task.

Judge clarity, distinctiveness, coherence across surfaces, feasibility and control. Precise density/status handling can distinguish a tool as expressive art distinguishes a gallery. Keep controls recognizable and accessible; let the concept earn its place through mutually supporting choices.

## Read references as mechanisms

Select a reference for a named quality, then actually view it at **whole surface → relationships → details**. Follow motion across its relevant duration and navigation across the linked task. Note what changed, what remained stable and where attention settled. A still identifies a moment; a sequence supports the observed transition.

Explain which visible relationships carry the quality and which depend on content, assets, timing or platform. A photographic composition may owe more to the subject and crop than the grid. Transfer the useful mechanism under its conditions, respecting source rights and the current identity. Revisit the hypothesis when new views or frames show a different result.

## Make decisions explainable and adjustable

Connect consequential choices to intent, observed evidence, inferred mechanism, implementation, tradeoff and recheck. A short existing record can suffice. Preserve useful frames or time ranges with the preview so the user can inspect the comparison; state what was actually viewed and what remains inferred.

Expose meaningful controls in tokens, content, variants, composition bounds, focal points or timing. Keep routine adjustments in code and reusable lessons with their applicable conditions.
